JOINTS IN THE STRUCTURES

Figure given is the typical frame structure. We solve such types of structures in "Strength of material" in Undergraduates. The major parameters require to be structural members are material property, sectional properties, boundary conditions also known as supports and loads.

Fig. Typical frame structure

Material property is governed by Modulus of Elasticity (\(E_s\)), where as sectional properties are governed by Area (\(A\)) and Moment of Inertia (\(I\)). Axial members such as truss, columns, struts etc require Area, whereas flexural members such as beam require Moment of Inertia . Loads are calculated and assigned with the procedures mentioned in the code or general practice. However, it is always complex while selecting the supports in the structures

Mostly, fixed supports in concrete section are comparatively easier than hinge supoort. By extending adequate amount of reinforcement with required development length might fulfill the requirement for fixed support but it is very difficult to make hinge support. Then how to make hinge support at the bottom. Lets have some discussion

We all know following properties of joints

So the joint in structure should follow the above criteria. The structure also also depends on how the loads tranfer.

1. SUPPORT (JOINT) AT THE BASE OF COLUMN.

In this case the support shall transfer moments, shear forces and axial forces. Most of the Structural Engineer designs the compression members (columns) and foundation (Either isolated, raft or pile), but not the joint between foundation and compression members.

Different codes give the load tranfer mechanism. As per IS 456:2000 (Clause 34.4),
the compressive stress in concrete at the base of a column or pedestal shall be considered as being transferred by bearing to the top of pedestal or footing. The bearing pressure on the loaded area shall not be exceeded the permissible bearing stress in direct compression multiplied by a value equal to

\(\sqrt(\frac{A_1}{A_2})\) but no greater than 2.

Where,

\(A_1\) = supporting area for bearing of footing, which in sloped or stepped footing may be taken as the area of the lower base of the largest frustum of a pyramid or cone contained wholly within the footing and having for its upperbase, the area actually loaded and having side slope of one vertical to two horizontal; and

\(A_2\) = loaded area at the column base.

\(A_1\) and \(A_2\) are illustrated in the figure below.

Fig. Column base

Assuming square column and footing

Area \(A_1 = \) max of (\(b_1^2\) & \(b_2^2\))

In the figure above, \(A_1 = b_2^2\)

\(A_2= b^2\)

Permissible bearing stress is given by

'
\(\sigma_b = 0.45 f_{ck} \sqrt(\frac{A_1}{A_2})\)

And actual bearing stresses shall be calculated by principle of bending,.

\(\sigma_{(x,y)} = \frac{P}{A}±\frac{M_x}{I_x}x±\frac{M_xy}{I_y}y\)

If \(\sigma_{(x,y)} > \sigma_b\), then the reinforcement should transfer the forces (axial, moments and even shear) to the foundation. In the practice, we extend all the reinforcement coming from column to the foundation. However, such types of connection is actually the fixed connection. Then, how can we make hinge connection?

We know that hinge connection have many advantages. In structural analysis point of view, it is easy to calculate. The section may be smaller in size and may require less reinforcement. The requirement of large section and reinforcement in columns are generally due to uniaxial or biaxial moments. So, again question is how to make the support hinge in concrete section.

In flexural member, moment of resistance is given by

\(M_R=0.362f_{ck}bx_uz\)
or
\(M_R=0.87f_yA_{st}z\)

In the above equations, if we consider \(z=0\) then moment of resitance, \(M_R=0\) then this section does not carry any moment in that direction. The section can transfer only tension (or compression) if \(z = 0\) in both direction. This condition is illustratred by following figure. Although it still may carry a small amount of moment.

In case of Steel Connection, the hinge connections are easily installed as given in the figure below.

However, still the connection between steel and concrete should be designed as the load coming from column to footing and that can be axial force, shear forces due to horizontal componenet of the axial loads.

In case of moment connection at the bottom of column, we have to make some arragement of bolts around the column so that the connection can be fixed. For fixed (moment) connection, the bolts shall carry moment by the relation \(M_i=P_iz_i\). That means the the bolts shall be sigficantly far from Neutral axis (\(z\) should be large. Mathematically, \(\sigma_{(x,y)} < \sigma_b\) and some anchors will have tensions (\(P_i\)) and they are far from neutral axis(\(z_i\)). The two types joint are differentiated by following figure.

2. JOINTS IN TRUSS MEMBERS

What is the difference between bolted conncection or welded connections in truss? It is not always necessary to become the members connected by bolts is pinned. However, they can be made pinned by providing suitable arrangement. However, the members connected by weld may carry moments and can not treat the connection as the hinge connection.

But due to the nature of truss the joint has very little or almost zero moments.

Pinned joints have not been assigned in this truss. In this truss, small moments have been observed in the members but they are significantly small that can be neglecting in the joint design and therefore can be designed as welded joint

However, if we provide joint with the release of moments, the joints don't have any moments and should be designed as bolted connection for axial loads only.

3. COLUMN-BEAM JOINTS

In concrete structure, moment connection have been provided in most cases by providing adequate reinforcement with development length.

IS 13920 - 2016: Ductile detailing of Reinforced Concrete Structures Subjected to Seismic Forces-Code of Practice , gives the detail of the requirement for the moment connection between beam and column. As per Clause 6.2.5, At an external joint, top and bottom bars of beams shall be provided with the anchorage length (\(X\)) beyond the inner face of the column, equal to development length of the bar in tension plus 10 times bar diameter minus the allowance for \(90^o\) bends

\(X=L_d+10\phi_t-R\)
